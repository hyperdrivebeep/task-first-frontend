## Layout
URL: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout

Universal rules:
- MUST order content by relative importance, placing the most important items near the top and leading side of the window or display | topic: visual hierarchy | source: Visual hierarchy | why: people start by reading from top to bottom and from leading to trailing side | check: the primary content and primary action sit in the top-leading region of each screen.
- MUST prefer standard components that adapt UI elements to the natural reading order of right-to-left languages | topic: right-to-left | source: Visual hierarchy | why: a fixed left-to-right layout breaks reading order for right-to-left users | check: right-to-left layout review uses standard components or equivalent adaptive behavior.
- MUST align elements so the layout is easier to scan | topic: alignment | source: Visual hierarchy | why: people assume aligned items are related and use alignment to track content while scrolling | check: layout review shows consistent alignment edges for related elements.
- MUST use indentation to show that an item is subordinate to the item it follows | topic: hierarchy | source: Visual hierarchy | why: people perceive indented items as subordinate | check: each indented item is a child of the item directly above it.
- MUST group related items with negative space, container shapes, or separator lines | topic: grouping | source: Visual hierarchy | why: grouping shows which elements are related and which are unrelated | check: each group of related items has a visible boundary or spacing cue.
- MUST use progressive disclosure to reduce the content and choices shown at first | topic: progressive disclosure | source: Visual hierarchy | why: too much content and too many choices make information harder to find | check: secondary content sits behind disclosure triangles, menus, nested views, or scrollable sections.
- MUST differentiate controls from content by using a scroll edge effect instead of a solid or semi-opaque background color beneath controls | topic: controls and content | source: Visual hierarchy | why: a distinct control layer keeps controls readable over scrolling content | check: no solid or semi-opaque background panel sits under controls that float over content.
- MUST extend full-screen background content underneath sidebars, toolbars, and tab bars so it fits the entire screen or window | topic: background | source: Visual hierarchy | why: background content that stops at the bars looks cut off | check: background content reaches every edge of the screen or window.
- MUST adapt the layout to display sizes, orientation changes, window sizes, and multitasking states | topic: adaptability | source: Adaptability | why: the environment changes how much space the app has | check: the layout is tested at each display size, orientation, window size, and multitasking state the app supports.
- MUST use an adaptive layout system so the interface responds to the device environment | topic: adaptability | source: Adaptability | why: the layout must respond to environment characteristics without manual rework | check: the layout is built with an adaptive layout mechanism rather than fixed positions.
- MUST handle different screen sizes, device orientations, and aspect ratios | topic: screen sizes | source: Adaptability | why: the same app runs on displays of many shapes | check: the layout is verified at each supported screen size and aspect ratio.
- MUST handle system features that occupy part of the screen | topic: system features | source: Adaptability | why: system features change the usable area of the screen | check: the layout accounts for each system feature the target devices have.
- MUST handle external displays and resizable windows | topic: windows | source: Adaptability | why: people add displays and resize windows during use | check: the layout works on an external display and at each supported window size.
- MUST respond to the system text-size setting | topic: text size | source: Adaptability | why: apps that ignore the setting can be difficult or impossible to use for people who rely on it | check: the app changes text size when the system text-size setting changes.
- MUST handle locale-based layout direction, date, time, and number formatting, font variation, and text length | topic: localization | source: Adaptability | why: each locale changes direction, formatting, and text length | check: each supported locale is previewed for direction, formatting, and text fit.
- MUST design a layout that adapts gracefully and consistently so the experience stays familiar on rotation, window resize, added display, or device switch | topic: consistency | source: Adaptability | why: people expect familiar behavior when the environment changes | check: the layout is reviewed across rotation, resize, added display, and device switch.
- MUST respect system-defined safe areas, margins, and guides where they are available | topic: safe areas | source: Adaptability | why: the system defines regions that keep content clear of system UI and hardware | check: content positions use the system safe area and margins.
- MUST consider layout modifiers to fine-tune the placement of views | topic: placement | source: Adaptability | why: modifiers give precise control over placement across sizes | check: placement adjustments are expressed through layout modifiers where the platform provides them.
- MUST keep the interface resizing well even when the app is locked to one orientation | topic: orientation lock | source: Adaptability | why: window and device sizes still vary in a locked orientation | check: a landscape-only layout is tested at each supported window size.
- MUST stack horizontally adjacent views vertically when larger text needs more space | topic: text size | source: Adaptability | why: side-by-side views leave too little room for text at larger sizes | check: at the largest text size, adjacent views stack vertically where needed.
- MUST let table rows and other containers grow in height so text is not cropped or overlapping other content | topic: text size | source: Adaptability | why: fixed heights crop larger text | check: at the largest text size, no row text is cropped or overlaps.
- MUST let rows with a single line of text grow to multiple lines when the text needs it | topic: text size | source: Adaptability | why: a one-line default can hide text at larger sizes | check: rows expand to show all lines at the largest text size.
- MUST preview the app on multiple devices, size classes, localizations, and text sizes | topic: testing | source: Adaptability | why: layout problems appear only in specific combinations | check: preview runs cover each device, size class, localization, and text size the app supports.
- MUST consider testing the largest and smallest layouts first | topic: testing | source: Adaptability | why: these extremes reveal clipping and overflow most quickly | check: the largest and smallest layouts are tested before the middle sizes.
- MUST consider testing on a simulated device to check for clipping and layout issues | topic: testing | source: Adaptability | why: simulated devices show layout problems before release | check: simulated device runs include a clipping review for resized layouts.
- MUST scale background artwork to fill the screen completely instead of changing its aspect ratio when the display changes | topic: background artwork | source: Adaptability | why: changing the aspect ratio distorts the artwork | check: background artwork fills the screen at every supported aspect ratio without distortion.
- MUST determine layout from the actual space available rather than from device type or orientation | topic: size classes | source: Size classes | why: device type and orientation do not describe the space the app has | check: layout code branches on available size, not device type or orientation.
- MUST consider every combination of size classes in both portrait and landscape aspect ratios | topic: size classes | source: Size classes | why: the app can appear in many size class combinations | check: each combination of width and height class is reviewed.
- MUST NOT design the layout for only one configuration, such as landscape with regular width and compact height | topic: size classes | source: Size classes | why: a single-configuration layout wastes space or fails in other configurations | check: the layout is reviewed for at least one configuration per size class combination.
- MUST NOT change the app's functionality based on the space it occupies | topic: functionality | source: Size classes | why: people expect the same features at every size | check: each feature is available at every supported size.
- MUST keep layout changes recognizable and familiar to the platform as size changes | topic: familiarity | source: Size classes | why: people expect the same layout language when they resize | check: layout changes at each size keep platform conventions.
- MUST consider using larger space to replace a tab bar with a sidebar or to expose functionality that would otherwise sit in an overflow menu | topic: navigation | source: Size classes | why: larger space can show navigation directly | check: each size change is reviewed for a navigation pattern that fits the space.
- MUST keep the layout recognizable and familiar for the device type the app is made for, even when the app is resized | topic: familiarity | source: Size classes | why: device type should not change while the layout adapts | check: resized layouts keep the device-type conventions.
- MUST consider using layout guides for standard margins and for restricting text width to improve readability | topic: readability | source: Guides and safe areas | why: standard margins and limited line length make text easier to read | check: text blocks use margin guides and a maximum width.
- MUST respect the safe area so system UI and hardware features do not obstruct content and controls | topic: safe areas | source: Guides and safe areas | why: obstructed content and controls are unusable | check: no content or control sits under system UI or hardware features in the safe area's excluded region.
- MUST NOT place controls or critical information at the bottom edge of a window | topic: window edges | source: Platform considerations | why: people often move windows so that the bottom edge is below the screen | check: no control or critical information is placed at the bottom edge.
- MUST NOT display content behind a hardware camera housing or cutout at the top edge of a window | topic: hardware cutouts | source: Platform considerations | why: content behind a cutout is hidden | check: top-edge content stays clear of hardware cutouts.
- MUST inset primary content from the screen edges so overscan or display settings do not crop it | topic: screen edges | source: Platform considerations | why: TV settings and overscan crop the edges of the screen | check: primary content sits inside the platform's inset margins.
- MUST include padding between focusable elements | topic: focus | source: Platform considerations | why: a focused element grows and can overlap its neighbors | check: spacing between focusable elements accounts for the enlarged focused size.
- MUST consider how elements look when focused and keep focused elements from overlapping important information | topic: focus | source: Platform considerations | why: focus states can cover content | check: each focused state is reviewed for overlap with important information.
- MUST use appropriate spacing between unfocused rows and columns so an item that comes into focus does not overlap others | topic: grids | source: Platform considerations | why: focus enlargement causes overlap in tight grids | check: grid spacing is verified in the focused state.
- MUST include additional vertical spacing for titled rows, between the previous row and the title and between the title and the row items | topic: grids | source: Platform considerations | why: crowded titles are hard to scan | check: titled rows have spacing above and below the title.
- MUST use consistent spacing across the layout | topic: consistency | source: Platform considerations | why: inconsistent spacing stops the content from looking like a grid and makes it harder to scan | check: spacing values repeat across rows and columns.
- MUST keep partially hidden offscreen content the same width on each side of the screen | topic: symmetry | source: Platform considerations | why: symmetry directs attention to the fully visible content | check: partially hidden content has equal visible width on both sides.
- MUST support resizing of windows and volumes where the app allows it, and adapt the layout as the size changes | topic: resizing | source: Platform considerations | why: resizing is standard behavior on spatial and desktop platforms | check: the layout is tested at multiple window and volume sizes.
- MUST prefer horizontally centered content at very large sizes so people can view and interact with it easily | topic: large sizes | source: Platform considerations | why: content at the edges of very large windows is hard to reach | check: content stays horizontally centered at the largest supported size.
- MUST NOT use minimum or maximum sizes to prevent resizing | topic: resizing | source: Platform considerations | why: resizing is expected behavior | check: no size limit blocks resizing entirely.
- MUST consider minimum and maximum sizes that keep elements from overlapping at small sizes and keep large layouts manageable | topic: size limits | source: Platform considerations | why: elements can overlap when space is tight and large layouts can become unwieldy | check: size limits are set for each window and attached element type.
- MUST use 3D content sparingly in windows and reserve it for meaningful moments alongside 2D content | topic: 3D content | source: Platform considerations | why: frequent 3D content competes with other content | check: 3D content appears only where it adds meaning next to 2D content.
- MUST place inline 3D content inset in the window so it does not collide with other content or appear outside the window edge | topic: 3D content | source: Platform considerations | why: unpredictable placement breaks the layout | check: inline 3D content sits inside the window bounds with clear space.
- MUST consider a volume or an immersive space for larger models or for views that consist mainly of 3D content | topic: 3D content | source: Platform considerations | why: larger 3D views need more room than a window provides | check: 3D-dominant views are reviewed for volume or immersive placement.
- MUST show supplemental content in an adjacent window instead of in a control overlay | topic: supplemental content | source: Platform considerations | why: overlay controls are for interactive controls, not supplemental content | check: supplemental content opens in a separate window beside the current one.
- MUST include enough space around controls so they are easy to identify and to interact with, and so hover effects do not obscure other content | topic: touch and gaze targets | source: Platform considerations | why: crowded controls are hard to target and hover effects cover neighbors | check: spacing between control centers is verified against the target size.
- MUST NOT place more than two or three controls side by side | topic: density | source: Platform considerations | why: crowded controls are hard to target on small screens | check: no row holds more than two or three controls side by side.
- MUST let text buttons span the full width of the screen, unless two short-label buttons fit on a screen that does not scroll | topic: buttons | source: Platform considerations | why: full-width buttons are easier to target | check: each button row is full width or fits a non-scrolling screen.
- MUST support autorotation for views that people may show to others | topic: orientation | source: Platform considerations | why: people may want to show content to someone else at a different orientation | check: views intended for sharing rotate with the device.

Persona and journey notes:
- People read from top to bottom and from leading to trailing side, so the most important content must come first.
- People rotate devices, resize windows, add displays, and switch devices, and expect the experience to stay familiar.
- People who rely on larger text need the layout to grow without cropping or overlap.
- People who move windows partly off the screen may not see content at the bottom edge.
