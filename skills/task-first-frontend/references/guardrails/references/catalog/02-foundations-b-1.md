# Exclusions and persona notes

### Inclusion
Apple-specific, excluded:
- SF Symbols glyph references, including nongendered, localization, and right-to-left glyphs
- Platform guide links (designing-for-ios, designing-for-ipados, designing-for-macos, designing-for-tvos, designing-for-visionos, designing-for-watchos, designing-for-games)
- Apple accessibility feature names used as examples (VoiceOver, Display Accommodations, Switch Control, Speak Screen)
- Apple developer documentation (localization in Xcode) and internal guideline IDs
Persona and journey notes:
- The page asks designers to examine the app through many different perspectives (listed in the universal rules above).
- New users benefit from a step-by-step onboarding path; experienced users can skip to content.
- Users may customize their own avatars, emoji, and characters, and may specify their pronouns.
- People with temporary and situational disabilities are treated as users who can be affected at any time.

### Layout
Apple-specific, excluded:
- Apple platform names, guide links, and developer documentation references (iOS, iPadOS, macOS, tvOS, visionOS, watchOS, designing-for links, related and developer links)
- Apple frameworks, APIs, and features named as examples (SwiftUI, Auto Layout, UIKit size classes, Dynamic Island, Liquid Glass, backgroundExtensionEffect, NSPrefersDisplaySafeAreaCompatibilityMode, isAutorotating, Unity accessibility plug-in)
- Platform numeric specifications (tvOS 60 and 80 pt margins, tvOS grid tables, visionOS 60 pt control spacing), with the principles kept in the rules above
Persona and journey notes:
- People read from top to bottom and from leading to trailing side, so the most important content must come first.
- People rotate devices, resize windows, add displays, and switch devices, and expect the experience to stay familiar.
- People who rely on larger text need the layout to grow without cropping or overlap.
- People who move windows partly off the screen may not see content at the bottom edge.

### Materials
Apple-specific, excluded:
- Liquid Glass name, its regular and clear variants, the 35% opacity dimming value, and the statement that tvOS image views and buttons adopt the material on focus (system behavior, not a designer rule)
- Apple APIs and classes named as examples (UIBlurEffect, UIVibrancyEffect, BlendingMode, Material, glassEffect, UIVisualEffectView, NSVisualEffectView, AVKit media controls)
- Related links, developer documentation, videos, and the change log
Persona and journey notes:
- People need to keep a sense of place, so background color should stay visible through materials.
- People who turn on reduced transparency or increased contrast need text on materials to stay legible.
- People in full-screen modal views need to tell controls apart from content at a glance.
