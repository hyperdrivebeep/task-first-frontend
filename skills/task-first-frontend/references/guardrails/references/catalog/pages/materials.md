## Materials
URL: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials

Universal rules:
- MUST use a material to visually separate foreground elements, such as text and controls, from background elements, such as content and solid colors | topic: separation | source: Intro | why: separation keeps foreground elements readable and shows hierarchy | check: foreground elements sit on a material layer that is distinct from background elements.
- MUST let background color pass through the material to the foreground so the material establishes visual hierarchy and helps people keep a sense of place | topic: hierarchy | source: Intro | why: see-through layers keep the user oriented in the surrounding content | check: the material is translucent enough for background color to show through.
- MUST place controls and navigation elements, such as tab bars and sidebars, in a distinct functional layer that floats above the content layer | topic: functional layer | source: Liquid Glass | why: a distinct layer makes the difference between interactive elements and content clear | check: controls and navigation float above content and are not mixed into it.
- MUST let content scroll and show through beneath floating controls and navigation while keeping those controls legible | topic: legibility | source: Liquid Glass | why: visible content adds depth, and controls must stay readable over it | check: scrolling content is visible under controls and the controls remain readable.
- MUST NOT place the floating functional-layer treatment in the content layer | topic: layer separation | source: Liquid Glass | why: using it in the content layer makes the visual hierarchy confusing | check: no content-layer element uses the floating-control treatment, except transient interactive controls covered by the next rule.
- MUST use standard materials for content-layer elements, such as app backgrounds | topic: content layer | source: Liquid Glass | why: standard materials keep the content layer distinct from the functional layer | check: content-layer backgrounds use standard materials.
- MUST give a transient interactive control in the content layer, such as a slider or toggle, the floating appearance only while a person activates it | topic: interactivity | source: Liquid Glass | why: the change signals that the element is interactive | check: the floating appearance appears only during activation.
- MUST use floating-material effects sparingly and limit them to the most important functional elements of a custom control | topic: restraint | source: Liquid Glass | why: strong effects draw attention away from the content | check: each custom control with the effect is one of the app's most important functional elements.
- MUST NOT overuse floating-material effects across multiple custom controls | topic: restraint | source: Liquid Glass | why: overuse distracts from the content and gives a subpar experience | check: the effect appears on only a few key controls across the app.
- MUST prefer standard system components, which pick up the appearance and behavior of the material automatically | topic: standard components | source: Liquid Glass | why: system components stay consistent without custom work | check: system components are used wherever they fit the need.
- MUST use the clear translucent variant only for components that appear over visually rich backgrounds, such as photos and videos | topic: variant choice | source: Liquid Glass | why: the clear variant is highly translucent and needs rich content behind it to read as intended | check: clear components sit only over visually rich backgrounds.
- MUST use the regular variant when background content could cause legibility problems or when a component has a significant amount of text, such as alerts, sidebars, and popovers | topic: variant choice | source: Liquid Glass | why: the regular variant blurs and adjusts background luminosity to keep text legible | check: text-heavy components and components over busy backgrounds use the regular variant.
- MUST add a dark dimming layer behind clear translucent components when the underlying content is bright | topic: contrast | source: Liquid Glass | why: a dimming layer restores contrast for foreground elements | check: bright backgrounds under clear components have a dimming layer.
- MUST NOT add a dimming layer behind clear translucent components when the underlying content is dark enough, or when standard media playback controls already provide their own dimming | topic: contrast | source: Liquid Glass | why: extra dimming is unnecessary and hides content | check: dark backgrounds and built-in media dimming get no extra layer.
- MUST check how translucent materials look under system settings that change transparency, contrast, or the preferred look | topic: accessibility | source: Liquid Glass | why: the appearance of these materials changes with those settings | check: the interface is reviewed with reduce transparency and increase contrast enabled.
- MUST use scroll edge effects beneath controls over scrolling content to improve legibility | topic: legibility | source: Liquid Glass | why: the effect blurs and reduces the opacity of background content under the controls | check: controls over scrolling content show the scroll edge effect.
- MUST use standard materials and effects to convey structure in the content layer beneath floating controls | topic: structure | source: Standard materials | why: standard materials show structure without competing with the functional layer | check: content-layer structure is built from standard materials and effects.
- MUST choose a material or effect based on its semantic meaning and recommended usage | topic: selection | source: Standard materials | why: meaning-based choices stay correct when the system changes the appearance | check: each material is justified by its intended use.
- MUST NOT choose a material or effect based on the apparent color it adds to the interface | topic: selection | source: Standard materials | why: system settings can change the appearance and behavior of a material | check: no material was picked for the color it imparts.
- MUST place vibrant colors on top of any material to keep foreground content legible | topic: vibrancy | source: Standard materials | why: vibrant colors stay legible on any material | check: text and symbols on materials use vibrant colors.
- MUST use system-defined vibrant colors on top of materials so colors stay legible in different contexts | topic: vibrancy | source: Standard materials | why: system colors adjust to the context, so they do not need manual checks for darkness, brightness, or saturation | check: foreground colors on materials come from the system's vibrant color set.
- MUST consider contrast and visual separation when combining a material with blur and vibrancy effects | topic: contrast | source: Standard materials | why: the material choice changes how well foreground elements separate from the background | check: each material and effect combination is reviewed for contrast and separation.
- MUST use thicker, more opaque materials for text and other elements with fine features that need more contrast | topic: thickness | source: Standard materials | why: more opacity gives fine features more contrast | check: text with fine features sits on the thicker material levels.
- MUST use thinner, more translucent materials where people need a visible reminder of the background content to keep their context | topic: thickness | source: Standard materials | why: visible background content helps people keep their place | check: thin materials are used where background context matters.
- MUST choose one of the four standard material thicknesses (ultra-thin, thin, regular, thick) in the content layer when visual distinction is needed | topic: thickness scale | source: Platform considerations: iOS, iPadOS | why: the thickness scale creates distinction within the content layer | check: each content-layer material is one of the four standard thicknesses.
- MUST use the system vibrant label, fill, and separator values that are designed for each material | topic: vibrancy | source: Platform considerations: iOS, iPadOS | why: the values are tuned to work with each material | check: label, fill, and separator colors on materials come from the system vibrant set.
- MUST use the label, secondary label, and tertiary label vibrancy levels for labels on any material | topic: vibrancy levels | source: Platform considerations: iOS, iPadOS | why: these levels keep useful contrast on every material | check: labels on materials use these levels, and the default label level carries the highest contrast.
- MUST NOT use the quaternary label vibrancy level on the thin or ultra-thin materials | topic: vibrancy levels | source: Platform considerations: iOS, iPadOS | why: the contrast is too low on these materials | check: no quaternary label sits on a thin or ultra-thin material.
- MUST use the fill vibrancy levels (fill, secondary fill, tertiary fill) for fills on all materials | topic: vibrancy levels | source: Platform considerations: iOS, iPadOS | why: these levels work on every material | check: fills on materials use these levels.
- MUST use the single default separator vibrancy value for separators on all materials | topic: separators | source: Platform considerations: iOS, iPadOS | why: the default value works well on every material | check: separators on materials use the default value.
- MUST use the standard macOS materials for their designated purposes | topic: purpose | source: Platform considerations: macOS | why: each standard material is designed for a specific role | check: each macOS material matches its designated purpose.
- MUST use the vibrant version of each color on macOS materials | topic: vibrancy | source: Platform considerations: macOS | why: vibrant colors keep foreground content readable on the material | check: colors on macOS materials use their vibrant versions.
- MUST decide when custom views and controls use vibrancy | topic: vibrancy | source: Platform considerations: macOS | why: vibrancy is applied by choice, not by default in every custom view | check: each custom view and control states whether vibrancy applies.
- MUST test the interface in a variety of contexts to find where vibrancy improves appearance and communication | topic: testing | source: Platform considerations: macOS | why: the effect of vibrancy depends on the background and settings | check: the interface is reviewed on several backgrounds and system settings.
- MUST choose a background blending mode, blending background content from behind the window or within the window, that complements the interface design | topic: blending | source: Platform considerations: macOS | why: the blending mode changes how background content mixes into the interface | check: the chosen blending mode is reviewed against the interface design.
- MUST use the ultra-thin material for full-screen views that require a light color scheme | topic: thickness by use | source: Platform considerations: tvOS | why: the thinnest material suits full-screen light views | check: full-screen light-scheme views use the ultra-thin material.
- MUST use the thin material for overlay views that partially obscure onscreen content and require a light color scheme | topic: thickness by use | source: Platform considerations: tvOS | why: a thin material keeps the content behind it visible in a light scheme | check: light-scheme overlays that partly cover content use the thin material.
- MUST use the regular material for overlay views that partially obscure onscreen content | topic: thickness by use | source: Platform considerations: tvOS | why: the regular material balances visibility of content with legibility of the overlay | check: partial overlays use the regular material.
- MUST use the thick material for overlay views that partially obscure onscreen content and require a dark color scheme | topic: thickness by use | source: Platform considerations: tvOS | why: the thick material keeps a dark overlay legible and distinct | check: dark-scheme overlays that partly cover content use the thick material.
- MUST use the system-defined window material as provided and MUST NOT modify it | topic: window material | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: the system material adapts to surroundings and keeps window contrast | check: window backgrounds use the unmodified system material.
- MUST let the window material adapt to the luminance of the objects and colors behind it instead of building a separate dark appearance | topic: adaptation | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: the material already adapts to the surroundings | check: no separate dark appearance is built for windows.
- MUST prefer translucency to opaque colors in windows | topic: translucency | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: translucency keeps people aware of the objects around them | check: windows use translucent areas where possible.
- MUST NOT use opaque areas that block people's view of virtual and physical objects around them | topic: translucency | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: opaque areas make people feel constricted and reduce their awareness | check: no opaque area blocks the surrounding view without need.
- MUST use the thin material for interactive elements, such as buttons and selected items, to draw attention to them | topic: thickness by use | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: a thin material highlights interactivity | check: buttons and selected items use the thin material.
- MUST use the regular material to visually separate sections of an app, such as a sidebar or a grouped table view | topic: thickness by use | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: the regular material separates sections without heavy contrast | check: sidebars and grouped sections use the regular material.
- MUST use the thick material for a dark element that stays visually distinct when it sits on top of a regular background | topic: thickness by use | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: the thick material keeps a dark element distinct | check: dark elements over regular backgrounds use the thick material.
- MUST apply vibrancy to text, symbols, and fills on materials so foreground content stays legible | topic: vibrancy | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: vibrancy pulls light and color forward from the surroundings | check: text, symbols, and fills on materials use vibrancy.
- MUST use the label vibrancy level for standard text | topic: vibrancy levels | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: the label level gives standard text the highest contrast | check: standard text uses the label level.
- MUST use the secondary label vibrancy level for descriptive text, such as footnotes and subtitles | topic: vibrancy levels | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: the secondary level marks descriptive text below the main text | check: footnotes and subtitles use the secondary label level.
- MUST use the tertiary label vibrancy level only for inactive elements, and only when the text does not need high legibility | topic: vibrancy levels | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: the tertiary level has the lowest contrast of the three | check: tertiary labels appear only on inactive elements with low legibility needs.
- MUST specify a system material for a custom component that needs visual separation or an indication of interactivity | topic: custom components | source: Platform considerations: visionOS | why: a system material gives the custom component a standard visual cue | check: each custom component that needs separation or interactivity names a system material.
- MUST use a material to give context in a full-screen modal view | topic: context | source: Platform considerations: watchOS | why: material layers help people orient themselves in a full-screen modal | check: full-screen modal views include a material layer.
- MUST use the contrast of material layers to help people distinguish controls and system elements from other content in full-screen modal views | topic: distinction | source: Platform considerations: watchOS | why: the layers separate controls from content | check: controls and system elements are distinguishable from other content in each modal.
- MUST NOT remove or replace the default material background of a modal sheet when the system provides it | topic: modal sheets | source: Platform considerations: watchOS | why: the default background keeps the modal's context and orientation | check: default modal sheet backgrounds are unchanged.

Persona and journey notes:
- People need to keep a sense of place, so background color should stay visible through materials.
- People who turn on reduced transparency or increased contrast need text on materials to stay legible.
- People in full-screen modal views need to tell controls apart from content at a glance.
